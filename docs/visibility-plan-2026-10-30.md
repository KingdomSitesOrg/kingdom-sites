# Being seen: the Kingdom Sites visibility plan (2026-10-30, T-382)

A checklist to keep open. Each item says what to do, who does it, how long it takes, and what to expect within about three months. Tick the box when it is done.

Thomas asked: *"What are some more tangible things I can do to be seen? I feel like it's impossible to ever get original natural clicks. Also what can I do to be shown on ChatGPT and Google AI? How can I dominate in that area as well?"* and *"I want anyone who searches Jam with Latin to also get my site somewhere near."*

## The honest starting point

- **About 800 visitors a year.** Google sent 27 clicks in nearly eight months (Search Console). Facebook sends about four times what Google does.
- **Natural clicks are possible, but slow.** Search builds over 6 to 12 months, not 3. A one-person studio wins mostly through what *other* sites and people say about it (reviews, listings, links, mentions), more than through its own blog posts.
- **There is already something to build on.** Google shows the site, just too low to be clicked: 61 times for Latin game searches, 23 times for "kingdom site", and 55 times for Rochester business searches. People already find Thomas on Google Maps.
- **Nobody "dominates" AI answers.** They change from one asking to the next: in one study, ChatGPT named the same top business only about 4% of the time for the same question ([summary](https://openlens.com/blog/en/how-ai-recommends-local-businesses-source-audit)). The realistic goal is to be **one of the names that keeps coming up**, and that is built from the same steps as below.

**What three months can realistically bring:** more calls and profile views from Maps, first clicks on Latin game searches, a few dozen more visitors a month, and perhaps one to three real enquiries that started on Google or in an AI answer. More than that comes later in the year, if the reviews and mentions keep coming.

## 2. AI search: what makes ChatGPT, Google AI and Perplexity name a business

### How each one picks

- **Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** use Google's normal index. Google says there are no extra requirements: a page must be indexed and able to show a snippet, and normal SEO is what counts ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). For local questions, the Business Profile and its reviews carry the weight. **Everything in §1 already serves Google AI.**
- **ChatGPT search** uses OpenAI's crawler, OAI-SearchBot, to decide which sites can appear. A site that blocks it is left out ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). kingdom-sites.com lets it in (checked on 30 October: robots.txt allows it, and the site answers it). To find pages, ChatGPT relies mostly on **Bing's index**, according to BrightLocal's study of 800 local searches. In that study, 58% of sources were business websites, 27% mentions on other sites and 15% directories ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-seo/uncovering-chatgpt-search-sources/)). Newer reports say ChatGPT cites Yelp heavily for local answers; the studies disagree, so treat that as a trend, not a rule.
- **Perplexity** has its own crawler, PerplexityBot, which can reach the site too. A study of 6.8 million AI citations across ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity found 86% came from sources the business controls, such as its website and listings ([Yext, via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251009106549/en)).

### Not worth the time

- **llms.txt.** Google says it does not use it, and Google's John Mueller said in 2025 that no AI system was using it ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-ai-llms-txt-39607.html), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-says-normal-seo-works-for-ranking-in-ai-overviews-and-llms-txt-wont-be-used-459422)). It takes ten minutes and does no harm, so it is optional, but expect nothing from it.
- **Paid "AI ranking" or "GEO" services** promising to put the business into ChatGPT. Nobody controls those answers, and the steps above are what an honest service would do anyway.

## 3. Showing up near searches for "JAM with Latin", "JAM with Latin app" and "Latin game"

**The site rule (updated 2026-10-30):** the site names the app, JAM Legio. It never mentions JAM with Latin except in the partnership credit shown with the game (and the app data on /latin-game).

**What is true today** (checked 30 October):
- **App Store:** the app is **JAM Legio**, sold by JAM with Latin LLC ($19.99). Its developer website link already points to **kingdom-sites.com/latin-game**.
- **Google Play:** the listing appears to link only to jamwithlatin.com.
- **jamwithlatin.com:** the home page carries no Kingdom Sites credit. It still sent 35 visitors this year, so a link exists somewhere on that site or from the app.
- **/latin-game:** has the partnership credit ("Made in partnership with JAM with Latin…"). It does not yet name the app or carry its structured data.

**What is realistic:** someone who searches "JAM with Latin" is looking for JAM with Latin. Google will show jamwithlatin.com, its social pages and the store listings first, and kingdom-sites.com is unlikely to rank on its own for someone else's brand name, especially when the rule keeps that name out of its titles. The realistic way to be "near" is to be **linked and credited on the pages that do show up for that search**: the App Store and Play listings and jamwithlatin.com itself. AI answers to "who made the JAM with Latin app?" can then say "built by Kingdom Sites", because the pages they read say so. For **"Latin game"**, kingdom-sites.com competes in its own right, and Google already shows /latin-game for it.
