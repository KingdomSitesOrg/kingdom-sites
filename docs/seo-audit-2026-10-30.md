# SEO and traffic audit: kingdom-sites.com (2026-10-30, T-382)

**Verdict:** almost nobody finds the site through search today. About 1 visitor in 20 comes from Google. Most visitors type the address or arrive from Facebook. The new site's code is now in good shape for search. Most of the room for more visitors is off the site: a Google Business Profile, a page for each project, and links from Thomas's own apps and clients. Before launch, one speed problem on the new site needs a decision: the error-reporting script makes pages slow on phones.

Audited: the live site (old design, `main`) and the new cinematic site (PR stack #39 > #40 > #41 > #43 > #44). The new site was audited on the preview at `dads-pc…:10075`, a production build of `t-376/what-i-built` merged with `t-375/intelligence`. The method was the `seo` skill: a phone Lighthouse sweep, then a check of the rendered HTML, the source, the redirects, the DNS and the structured data.

## 2. Technical SEO

### Blocking: costing visitors on the new site (fixed on PR #44's branch)

| Problem | What it cost | Evidence (preview, before the fix) | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharing /about, /portfolio, /get-started, /privacy or /terms showed the home page's **old** card ("Kingdom Sites — mobile application solutions", old description), with the share address set to the home page | Facebook is the #2 source. A shared portfolio link looked like the old home page, and Facebook could group it with the home page | `og:url = https://kingdom-sites.com` and the old `og:title` on all five (page-audit) | Each page has its own share card (`shareCard()` in `src/lib/seo.ts`). The root fallback now uses the current home wording and no address |
| /ai-tooling, /blog and /ruta shared with **no picture** | A bare link gets far fewer clicks on Facebook and LinkedIn | `hasOgImage: false`. These pages declared their own `openGraph`, which replaces the inherited picture | They list the site picture again |
| /mission had **two main headings** (`h1`): the film's title and the page's own | Google is less sure what the page is about | page-audit `h1Count: 2` | The film title is now a sub-heading (`h2`), with the same look |

### Real gaps (fixed)

- **"Kingdom Sites" twice in titles:** "About — Kingdom Sites | Kingdom Sites", and the same on Mission, Privacy and Terms. Fixed. About is now "About Thomas Klein | Kingdom Sites", which also names the person people search for.
- **Privacy and Terms reused the site's old description.** They now have their own.
- **/local-business answered 307 (temporary).** That tells Google to keep the old address indexed. It now answers 308 (permanent), like /seo, /software and /rochester-mn.
- **/refer is in the menu but was not in the sitemap.** Added.
- **Structured data** (the hidden facts a search engine reads):
  - The business entry (`ProfessionalService`) still described the old offer ("Ongoing product engineering for founders"). It now uses the home page's description, has an `@id`, a picture, and **Rochester, MN, US** as its city and state. There is no street address, phone, opening hours or rating, because the site publishes none.
  - Added **Thomas as a Person** on /about.
  - Added **Tap to Tick as a MobileApplication** (free, iOS 17+, finance, App Store link). It cannot earn star ratings in search until there is a published rating; none was invented.

### Checked and correct

- **Canonical tags:** every public page points at itself; none inherit the home page's. (The root layout already avoids that trap.)
- **sitemap.xml and robots.txt:** both served. The sitemap lists the public pages and the six blog posts. robots.txt blocks /sign, /billing, /feedback and the like.
- **Pages that must stay hidden:** /full-potential is live with `noindex, nofollow`. It is not in the sitemap and not blocked in robots.txt, so Google can read the noindex. Correct. /calc, /feedback, /farzana*, the referral tracker and /r/ short links are all noindex.
- **Redirects:** on the new site, /my-work and /work go to /portfolio (308), and /work/farzana goes to /portfolio#farzana. /my-work gets 51 visitors a quarter on the live site, so that redirect matters on launch day. A missing page returns a real 404.
- **One h1 per page** on every other public page. **Image alt text:** no image is missing it.
- **HTTPS** redirects correctly, and the certificates are valid for apex and www.
- **hreflang:** not needed. /ministry offers Bangla videos, but the page itself is in English.

### Still open: the new site

1. **Speed on phones. Fix before launch.** Phone Lighthouse, lab numbers:

   | Page | Live (old) perf / LCP / TBT | New preview perf / LCP / TBT |
   |---|---|---|
   | Home | 79 / 3.4 s / 510 ms | 39 / 5.0 s / 1,970 ms |
   | About | 47 / 6.4 s / 3,500 ms | 40 / 10.4 s / 7,260 ms |
   | Portfolio (/my-work live) | 79 / 3.4 s / 430 ms | 45 / 4.1 s / 8,030 ms |
   | Get started | 89 / 3.0 s / 270 ms | 54 / 4.5 s / 2,190 ms |
   | AI consultation | 64 / 3.4 s / 2,080 ms | 74 / 4.0 s / 480 ms |
   | Latin game | 94 / 2.9 s / 100 ms | 59 / 4.7 s / 860 ms |

   CLS (layout shift) is 0 everywhere, so the cinematic motion does not make the page jump. The problem is blocking time, which is the lab stand-in for INP (how fast a tap responds).
   - **The main cause is one 559 KB script, mostly Sentry error reporting including Session Replay** (the `rrweb` recorder). It costs 4 to 5 seconds of phone CPU on /about and /portfolio. `instrumentation-client.ts` turns Replay on for every visitor (`replaysSessionSampleRate: 0.1`, `replaysOnErrorSampleRate: 1.0`, which keeps the recorder running for everyone). It is the same on the live site; the new pages make it worse. **Decision for Thomas:** load Replay only after the page is idle, or turn session replays off and keep plain error reports. Not changed here, because it changes what the monitoring records.
   - **Product screenshots in browser frames are sent full size to phones:** `unoptimized` 1,800 px JPEGs (`src/components/cinematic/Frames.tsx`, `Browser`). Lighthouse estimates about 100 KB wasted per image on /portfolio. The phone frames already have resized AVIF/WebP copies (`scripts/phone-shots.mjs`); give the browser frames the same treatment.
   - Caveat: the preview is served from Dad's PC over Tailscale (about 300 ms to first byte, against 30 to 40 ms on Vercel), and the Mac was shared with other jobs. The new-site numbers are therefore worse than real phones will see. The ranking between pages, and the cause, hold. Real-visitor data (Vercel Speed Insights) was not read; see §7.
2. **The home page title carries no search words:** "Kingdom Sites — Made to be used. Made to be yours." The title is the strongest signal on the page. Wording is Thomas's call (T-381). A suggestion: *"Kingdom Sites — websites, apps and software for businesses and churches"*, keeping the tagline as the visible heading. The same applies to "Portfolio | Kingdom Sites"; for example *"Portfolio: apps, school software and websites by Thomas Klein"*.
3. **Leftover keywords** in the root layout ("fractional CTO", "software for founders") describe the old offer. Google ignores this tag, so it costs nothing. Tidy it when convenient.

### Live site (old design): what hurts it today

The same speed issue (Sentry Replay), the same doubled-brand titles, and pages sharing the old home card. /work returns 404 there. Since deploys are on hold until the new site ships, none of this is worth fixing separately on `main`. It all lands with the stack.

### Nice to have

- **Apex vs www:** `www.kingdom-sites.com` serves its own copy of the site (200) instead of redirecting. Every page's canonical tag points at the apex, so Google already knows which one counts. A redirect in Vercel's Domains settings makes it clean (Thomas, dashboard).
- **Sitemap dates:** every static page says it changed at build time (`lastModified: new Date()`). Google learns to ignore such dates. Real dates per page would be better.
- **/sign** is both noindex and blocked in robots.txt, so Google cannot see the noindex. No harm unless someone links to it publicly.

## 3. Content and keywords: what the site could realistically win

No keyword-volume tool was available. The following is judgement, based on how competitive each search is and what the site can prove.

| Search people make | Realistic? | What would earn it |
|---|---|---|
| Product names: "Tap to Tick", "Farzana Classroom", "Latin practice game for homeschool" | **Yes, soonest.** /latin-game already gets Google visits | Keep a strong page for each, link to it from the App Store and Play listings, and add structured data (Tap to Tick done; the Latin game needs its name and price from Thomas) |
| "app developer Rochester MN", "web developer Rochester MN", "software developer near me" | **Yes, if there is a Google Business Profile.** Page changes do not win the map pack; the profile, reviews and categories do | A Business Profile, plus one real service page for Rochester (today /rochester-mn only redirects home) |
| "church website designer", "church app developer", "app for my church" | Head terms: hard (national agencies). Long tail: yes | A page "Websites and apps for churches", with /prayer and /ministry as proof, and a short article such as "What a small church actually needs from a website" |
| "custom school software", "software for a homeschool co-op / tutoring school", "class sign-up and payments for schools" | Long tail: yes | A Farzana Classroom case study on kingdom-sites.com linking to farzana.app, and farzana.app linking back |
| "AI consultation for businesses", "AI training for teams", "Claude / AI agents consultant" | **Yes, a young niche.** /ai-tooling is short (309 words) | Expand the page (who it is for, what a session covers, outcomes), plus two or three how-to articles from real sessions. The wording says *businesses*, not *small businesses*, as the site does |
| "Rochester MN small business" (the six existing blog posts) | They rank for little: no Google visit landed on the blog in 3 months | Keep them (harmless local signal), but new writing should serve the new offer |

**How the portfolio can rank:** today /portfolio is one long page with sections, so only one address can rank for all of it. Give each project its own page, for example `/portfolio/farzana-classroom`, `/portfolio/latin-game`, `/portfolio/tap-to-tick`, `/portfolio/kcupg`, `/portfolio/delta`, `/portfolio/prayer`. Each would have its own title, one h1, the problem, what was built, the result (a number where there is one), screenshots with alt text, and a link to the live product. Keep /portfolio as the index. Every case study is also something to post on Facebook, the site's second-largest source.
